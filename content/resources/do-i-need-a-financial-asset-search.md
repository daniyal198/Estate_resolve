If you are confident you already know about every bank account, pension, and investment the deceased held, a formal search may not be necessary. However, many families discover previously unknown accounts, especially where the deceased held accounts with several institutions over a long life, or where paperwork has been lost.

A search is most often recommended when the deceased's financial affairs were not well documented, or when the executor wants to demonstrate that reasonable steps were taken to identify all estate assets.
