# How to Find All the Assets of a Deceased Person in the UK: A Complete Guide for Executors

When someone dies, one of the executor's most important responsibilities is to identify all of the deceased's assets and liabilities before the estate can be administered correctly.

Many executors assume they already know everything the deceased owned. In reality, it is surprisingly common for assets to be overlooked. Bank accounts, investments, pensions and insurance policies may have been forgotten over many years, particularly if the deceased moved house, changed employers or dealt with several financial institutions.

Missing even one significant asset can delay the administration of an estate and, in some circumstances, expose an executor to criticism if reasonable enquiries were not made.

## Does an Executor Have a Duty to Search for Assets?

Yes. An executor or administrator has a duty to make reasonable enquiries to identify the deceased's estate.

This does not mean searching indefinitely or incurring unnecessary expense, but it does mean taking sensible steps to establish what the deceased owned and owed before distributing the estate.

The more complex the estate, the greater the level of investigation that may be appropriate.

## Assets That Are Commonly Missed

Some of the most frequently overlooked assets include:

* Bank and building society accounts.
* Premium Bonds and National Savings products.
* Shareholdings.
* Workplace and personal pensions.
* Life insurance policies.
* Investment portfolios.
* ISAs.
* Online investment accounts.
* Cryptocurrency.
* Overseas bank accounts.
* Business interests.
* Refunds and compensation payments.
* Unclaimed dividends.

Many of these assets remain undiscovered simply because no paperwork is found at the deceased's home.

## Where Should Executors Start?

A logical approach usually includes:

1. Reviewing personal papers and financial records.
2. Checking recent bank statements.
3. Reviewing tax returns.
4. Contacting known financial institutions.
5. Looking for evidence of investments or pensions.
6. Reviewing email accounts for financial correspondence.
7. Searching for property ownership.
8. Considering whether a professional asset search is appropriate.

Working methodically helps reduce the risk of missing important information.

## What Happens If Assets Are Missed?

If an estate is distributed before all assets are identified, additional work may be required later to recover funds or make further distributions.

Beneficiaries may receive less than they are entitled to until the missing assets are discovered.

Carrying out reasonable searches before distribution is generally far easier than trying to correct matters afterwards.

## When Should You Consider a Professional Asset Search?

A professional search may be particularly useful where:

* The deceased managed their own finances.
* Records are incomplete.
* The deceased moved frequently.
* There may be overseas assets.
* There are multiple beneficiaries.
* The estate is of significant value.
* The executor wants reassurance that reasonable enquiries have been made.

Professional searches are designed to complement the executor's own enquiries, not replace them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long should an executor spend searching for assets?

There is no fixed time limit. The appropriate level of investigation depends on the circumstances of the estate and the information available.

### Can assets still be found years after someone dies?

Yes. Forgotten bank accounts, investments and other financial assets are sometimes discovered long after an estate has been administered.

### Are overseas assets common?

They are more common than many people expect, particularly where the deceased worked abroad, owned holiday property or emigrated during their lifetime.
